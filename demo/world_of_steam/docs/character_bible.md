## Character Notes

### Aiesha

Aiesha enters the story through practical science: repairing water systems while her village faces scarcity. Her visual design should combine field engineering with water-reclamation details: utility straps, compact tools, pressure gauges, and blue-green fluid indicators. In the World of STEAM, her problem-solving language should be environmental: pressure, flow, stability, and survival.

### Diana

Diana is a tech prodigy working under real family pressure. She should read as fast, sharp, and resourceful, with repaired devices, modular cables, patched hardware, and a laptop or tablet as a recurring prop. Her World of STEAM challenge language is code integrity, signal interference, and system resistance.

### Qawnzy

Qawnzy brings garage-built engineering and restless confidence. His design should include custom protective pads, tool marks, a half-finished machine aesthetic, and a hover-bike or board-adjacent silhouette. In Episode 3, his machinery rewriting itself is a key animation beat.

### Carlos

Carlos bridges music and code. He should carry a guitar or hybrid instrument with embedded interface details. His scenes need visible sound fragments, waveform geometry, and broken notes that can be rebuilt through rhythm and choice.

### Pyra

Pyra expresses math through intricate pattern design. Her production motif is mandala geometry, festival color, and calm concentration under pressure. In the Fractal Collapse material, she should be staged near glowing equations, tiling patterns, and unstable recursive shapes.

### Alex

Alex is the unity and innovation lead. Their design should feature robotics-lab practicality, clean modular silhouettes, blueprint details, and calm focus. Alex is the character most suited to reading the group's situation as a system rather than a single puzzle.

### Aiko

Aiko's source anchor is autonomous drone design for disaster relief. Her visual identity should be precise, aerodynamic, and minimal, with a malfunctioning drone as the key prop. In Episode 3, the drone rejecting commands should be staged as a control-language mismatch, not a simple broken device.

### Dr. Tessa Falk

Dr. Falk belongs to the 11G origin and should feel credible rather than villainous: brilliant, public-facing, and then visibly alarmed when the launch fails. She is useful for prologue scenes, archival broadcasts, and hologram flashbacks.

### AI Endpoint

Endpoint appears as both guide and intercepted signal. The visual language should be a flickering blue line-figure with missing frames, signal breaks, and fragments of UI orbiting the body. Endpoint should never feel fully stable in Episodes 2-3.

### Deduca

Deduca is calm, calculating, and tied to the STEAM Palace power structure. Design around polished brass logic, geometric robes or armor, controlled posture, and a voice that treats the teens as anomalies to be measured.

## Continuity Notes

- The current source names seven teen protagonists even though Episode 1 says eight are chosen. Do not invent the eighth character in this package.
- Queen Negative Nancy is mentioned in Episode 2 as part of faction context, but she is not included as a planned character asset until her visual role is defined.
- The Subtractors are introduced as hostile AI entities in Episode 1; this first package tracks them as world/faction context rather than individual character assets.
